# Speaker script (~6 minutes)

Use with speaker notes in the deck. Do not mention match results, rankings, or awards.

---

**Slide 1 — Title (40s)**  
Today I’m talking about **Enzo** — Team 1577 Steampunk’s robot for FRC **Crescendo**. This is a design argument. We’re not looking at scoreboards. We’re asking: which robot best solved Crescendo’s engineering problem?

**Slide 2 — Meet Enzo (35s)**  
Enzo is Steampunk’s Crescendo machine from Aviv High School in Raanana. Crescendo is about Notes, Speaker, Amp, and Stage. Our lens is strategy clarity, mechanism fit, adaptability, and alliance value.

**Slide 3 — Demands (45s)**  
Crescendo rewarded robots that could acquire Notes under contact, cycle Speaker while still helping Amp, show up for Stage, move through defense, and give drivers software they trust. One-trick robots weren’t enough.

**Slide 4 — Definition (40s)**  
“Best” means: clear strategy, high cycle quality, role flexibility, courage to iterate, and driver trust. That’s our rubric.

**Slide 5 — Rebuild (40s)**  
After early district play, Steampunk chose a substantial redesign. Enzo’s public rebuild story shows elite culture: when the field disagrees with CAD, you change the robot. That discipline is rarer than clever parts.

**Slide 6 — Notes (35s)**  
Possession is power. Best Crescendo robots make intake forgiving and handoff automatic. Enzo’s architecture is organized around uninterrupted Note flow so every possession becomes an alliance asset.

**Slide 7 — Scoring (40s)**  
Speaker threat plus Amp utility. Dual-mode scoring gives drivers choices under congestion. Enzo’s claim is versatility in chaos — not a single highlight shot.

**Slide 8 — Endgame (35s)**  
Climbing only matters if teleop still works. Enzo is stage-ready without compromising the scoring identity alliances need for most of the match.

**Slide 9 — Software (40s)**  
Hardware sets the ceiling; controls decide whether you reach it. State-aware workflows, vision, and honest autonomous feedback make hard actions feel ordinary — and strategy expands when drivers trust the machine.

**Slide 10 — Why Enzo (50s)**  
Five reasons: fits the real problem; rebuilt with courage; prioritizes possession; stays versatile; closes the loop with software. That’s why Enzo is the best — by design.

**Slide 11 — Close (30s + Q&A)**  
Enzo is best because it was designed to be. Not because of a bracket — because Steampunk built clear strategy, fearless iteration, and a robot drivers and alliances can trust. Questions?
