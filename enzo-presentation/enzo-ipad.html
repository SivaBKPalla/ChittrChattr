ENZO

Why Enzo is a great robot — how it was built, and why that matters

A short talk about the robot itself

Who built Enzo?

Meet the builders

The team

High school students on Team 1577 (Steampunk) built Enzo in Raanana, Israel.

The robot

Enzo is their Crescendo robot — made to pick up Notes, score, and climb.

Our focus

We’ll talk about how they built it and why those choices make Enzo so good.

How they built it

They planned, built, then improved

  • They started with a clear plan for what Enzo should do well
  • They built the big parts to work together, not as random add-ons
  • When something was weak, they took parts apart and rebuilt them
  • That “fix it until it’s right” habit is a big reason Enzo is strong
Benefit #1

Built to grab Notes fast

How

Enzo’s intake is made to pick Notes up quickly and hold them so they don’t fall out.

Why that’s good

If you can’t grab Notes, you can’t score. A strong grabber means less wasted time and fewer mistakes.

Benefit #2

Built to score in more than one way

How

Enzo can shoot into the Speaker and also score in the Amp for its partners.

Why that’s good

Drivers can pick the better option when the field is crowded. One-trick robots get stuck. Enzo stays useful.

Benefit #3

Built to climb without hurting scoring

How

The climb was added in a way that doesn’t wreck the parts used for scoring.

Why that’s good

Some robots get worse at scoring because climbing was bolted on too late. Enzo keeps both jobs working.

Benefit #4

Built so drivers can trust it

How

The computer code helps with aiming, simple controls, and cameras that help Enzo “see.”

Why that’s good

Hard jobs feel easier. Drivers can focus on the match instead of fighting the robot.

Benefit #5

Built as one full robot

  • Drive, grabber, shooter, climb, and code were planned together
  • That means parts help each other instead of fighting each other
  • The robot feels complete — not like a pile of half-finished ideas
  • That’s what makes a design feel “best,” not just cool-looking
