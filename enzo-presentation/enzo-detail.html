Team 1577 Steampunk

ENZO

Why this robot is so good

The team

Built by students

Team 1577 is called Steampunk. They are high school students from Raanana, Israel. They design, build, wire, and program their own robot.

The robot

Enzo is their Crescendo robot

Enzo is the machine they built for the Crescendo season. Its job is to pick up Notes, score them, and climb.

Notes

Notes are foam rings

The game pieces are orange foam rings called Notes. They sit on the floor and also come from human players. Enzo has to grab them cleanly before it can score.

How the team worked

They rebuilt mid-season

After early events, Steampunk took Enzo apart and rebuilt big sections. They changed the robot when the first version was not good enough.

Drive

Wheels that can move any direction

Enzo uses swerve drive. Each wheel corner can point and roll the way the driver needs. That helps it turn fast, slide sideways, and line up for intakes or shots.

Intake — how

Front rollers spin and pull

On the front of Enzo are spinning rollers. When the robot drives into a Note, the rollers catch the foam ring and pull it up into the robot.

Intake — detail

The Note stays trapped inside

After the rollers grab the Note, the path inside holds it so it does not bounce back onto the floor. Drivers can keep moving while the Note is secured.

Inside the robot

Note path from front to shooter

The Note travels from the front rollers through a short path to the shooter wheels. That handoff is what lets Enzo go from pickup to shot without stopping to reload by hand.

Shooter — Speaker

Flywheels launch Notes into the Speaker

Spinning shooter wheels fling the Note into the Speaker, the big goal. Enzo can do this after the Note has been pulled in from the floor.

Shooter — Amp

Same robot can score the Amp

Enzo can also place a Note into the Amp for its partners. That second path means the drivers can choose Speaker or Amp based on what the field looks like.

Climb — how

Hooks catch the chain

Near the end of a match, Enzo can hook onto the stage chain and lift itself up. The climb hardware is built onto the same robot that still scores Notes.

Climb — detail

Climbing did not kill scoring

The climb was fitted so the intake and shooter still work during the match. Scoring stays usable until it is time to hang.

Cameras

Sensors help Enzo see the field

Cameras on the robot watch tags and goals on the field. That data helps Enzo aim and know where it is while driving.

Code

Software keeps controls simple

Code on the robot and driver station handles aiming helpers and button actions. Drivers use controllers; the code does the fiddly timing and targeting work.

Why the design works

Every part feeds the next

  • Swerve lines Enzo up for pickups and shots
  • Intake pulls Notes in and holds them
  • Path moves Notes to the shooter
  • Shooter can hit Speaker or Amp
  • Climb hangs without removing those jobs
  • Cameras and code make the drivers’ job easier
Close
