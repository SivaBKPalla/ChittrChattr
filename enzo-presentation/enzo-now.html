Team 1577 Steampunk

ENZO

Why this robot is so good

Not the scores. Just how they built it.

Quick intro

Enzo is Steampunk’s robot

Team 1577 is a high school robotics team from Raanana, Israel. Enzo is the robot they built for Crescendo.

I’m going to walk through how the main parts work, then why those choices matter.

How the team worked

They didn’t freeze the first design

Mid-season, they ripped out big chunks of Enzo and rebuilt them.

That’s not failure. That’s the team noticing “this part isn’t good enough” and actually fixing it. A lot of teams won’t do that.

The intake

Rollers in front pull Notes inside

Notes are the foam rings on the floor. Enzo has spinning rollers on the front. Drive into a Note, and those rollers yank it up into the robot and keep it from falling back out.

So drivers don’t have to baby the robot into the perfect spot. They can scoop Notes while still moving. That’s the whole point of a good intake.

The shooter

Same robot, two scoring paths

Once a Note is inside, Enzo can shoot it into the Speaker, or place it in the Amp for its partners.

Having both options matters when the field gets packed. You’re not stuck doing only one thing.

The climb

They fitted climbing around scoring

Enzo can hang on the chain at the end. The important part is how they did it: the climb doesn’t wreck the scoring setup.

Some teams bolt on a climb late and suddenly the robot is worse at everything else. Enzo was put together so scoring still works the whole match.

The code

The computer side does the annoying work

Cameras and code help Enzo aim and keep controls simple for the drivers.

So the people driving aren’t wrestling the robot every second. They can actually play the match.

Why it adds up

The parts were built to fit together

  • Intake feeds the shooter cleanly
  • Shooter can still work even with a climb on the robot
  • Code makes those parts easier to use in a real match
  • And when something was weak, the team rebuilt it
So yeah

That’s why Enzo is the best.

Good intake. Flexible scoring. Climb that doesn’t ruin the rest. Code drivers can live with. And a team that kept fixing it.

1 / 10
